# Instrument Idea

For our first NIME exercise, my group imagined an instrument made from a web of threads. The performer stands at the center. The audience sits or stands around the outside edge, each person in contact with the perimeter. When the performer moves—plucking, sweeping, tugging—the motion travels as vibrations along the threads, radiating outward to everyone at the boundary.

### Design constraints and inspirations


- **World**: hear sound with other parts of the body. We leaned into haptics and vision rather than air‑borne sound.
- **Social**: performed in public. The circular layout keeps the audience present and co‑located with the instrument.
- **Aesthetics**: symmetry. The radial web creates mirrored paths for motion and force.
- **Purpose**: sustenance. The center‑to‑edge breathing of the web suggests a pulse or heartbeat the group can entrain to.


- **You are**: broadcast music. Movements at the hub are literally “broadcast” as traveling waves across the medium.

A science‑fiction anchor for us was the idea of a social species communicating through a shared web—signals propagating to the edges as language and music.

### Mapping gesture to sensation


- **Input**: whole‑body gestures at the center node. Hands, arms, torso, even stepping patterns.
- **Medium**: tensioned threads form a radial and circumferential lattice. Different strands have varied thickness and tension to shape frequency content and decay.


- **Output**: multimodal feedback at the rim. Vibrations are felt through touch. Visual micro‑motions shimmer across the lattice. Quiet acoustic rustle accompanies the motion for a hybrid tactile‑auditory experience.

This instrument reframes musical expression as distributed sensation rather than loudness. Gesture‑to‑sensation mapping is legible at room scale and doesn’t depend on screens or speakers.
